# Observation 189 — separate choice, commitment, and funding posture

## Pressure

Observation 188 separated spending description from decision framing. A fixed or recurring cost exposes another conflation: `need`, `want`, `prepare`, and `committed` do not all answer the same question.

A recurring subscription can be both wanted and already committed for the next payment. Groceries can be needed without a particular purchase already being committed. Preparing money is a funding action toward a future use, not a moral property of the use.

## Candidate shape

```text
spending description
× choice (need / want)
× commitment state
× funding posture
```

## Lean witnesses

The observation proves that:

- committed does not imply need;
- need does not imply committed;
- need and committed can coexist;
- changing funding posture preserves description, choice, and commitment.

## Boundary

All names remain observation-local. This does not establish a binary moral taxonomy, legal necessity, a rule to cut wants first, a Capacity migration, a routing change, or a recommendation engine.

The practical implication is narrower: `fixed cost` or `Scheduled` evidence may establish commitment/timing, but it must not silently determine the household's need/want judgment.
